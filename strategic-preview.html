Timeless Investments, a brand of New Horizon GmbH (Berlin, founded 2018, 45 staff), makes rare collectibles investable through fractional ownership: watches, cars, art, wine, trading cards. On 28 September it soft-launched Collectible Invst, a bearer bond with an ISIN, a €1,000 minimum and a term of roughly eight years.

Product and trust

  • Eight years of operations, regulated in DACH, the strongest asset operations in the category.
  • A track record that sells itself: 75+ exits at 15% gross annualized.
  • Collectible Invst gives the category an ISIN that advisers and brokers can carry.
  • The 2023 shift toward investment-savvy, long-term customers is the right one.

Audience and assets

  • 400,000 registrations, 80,000 buyers, and regular interaction with 20 to 25K people. The audience of a much larger company.
  • Five years of asset content already produced, never packaged.
  • The best converters are clear: alternative-investment seekers with a collectibles interest and a long-term mindset.
  • The Sparplan already matches default DACH investing behaviour (Trade Republic, Scalable).

Acquisition and conversion

  • Five agencies tried to make paid CAC and ROAS work. None did, and mass outreach brought in less liquid investors.
  • Revenue per user is the lowest in the category. The gap is conversion and ticket size, not reach.
  • 67,500 lapsed buyers have never been segmented or worked.
  • Long holds mean investors rarely get capital back, so they rarely have a reason to reinvest.
  • The collectors' club is not leveraged enough, and the referral and affiliate programme is standard and not gamified.

Content and channels

  • In-house capacity covers LinkedIn and Instagram only. No YouTube, no search or answer-engine presence.
  • Content is announcement-led, not proof-led. Exits and distributions are not headlined.
  • No adviser or distribution partner channel for the ISIN yet.
  • Collectible Invst is pre-audience and currently marketed to the existing base.

Collectible Invst now qualifies the right investor. Nothing currently reaches them.

A €1,000 minimum on an eight-year bond does the filtering that five performance agencies could not buy their way to. What is not solved is distribution. The bond has no audience of its own, and marketing it to the existing base cannibalises rather than grows.

Where the opportunity sits

In what Timeless already owns: an audience of 80,000 buyers, an archive nobody has packaged, and the first product in the category an adviser can put in a portfolio. The strategy reactivates the lapsed base, turns the archive into a content engine, and gives Collectible Invst its own distribution through advisers.

Every workstream is marked runnable in-house, needs outside hands, or not yet. Scoped to fifteen people with two on marketing.

The product already filters them. The plan is about reaching them: advisers and distribution partners carrying the ISIN, not more paid targeting.

Content and narrative first, then the channels that carry it, then the spend.

Treated as a channel in its own right, with a format system and a production plan two people can hold.

Proof-led content built on what you already have: 75+ exits at 15% gross annualized, and the asset class beating the S&P in 21 of 22 down quarters.

The Sparplan and the bond's annual payouts, positioned as the portfolio mechanic the product has been missing.

Measured on qualified investors and average investment. Never on registrations.

The lapsed base is the only pool large enough, and nobody has segmented it. That is where the plan starts.

67,500 people trusted Timeless with capital once. The right-profile share of them is the cheapest qualified investor Timeless will ever acquire. Phase 1 brings them back with proof, not promotions, and builds the content that makes Timeless the answer when someone in DACH asks how to invest in collectibles.

Lapsed base reactivation

The core lever. Segmented, sequenced, measured per investor.

  • Email and CRM sequences per segment: exits, distributions, new drops by category
  • Sparplan as the re-entry product: small recurring ticket, portfolio logic
  • Personal outreach to the high-value lapsed segment
  • Win-back offers tested on cohorts, never blanket

Archive content engine

Five years of asset content, finally put to work. Malte's idea, built into a system.

  • YouTube storytelling series: the asset, the story, the exit
  • Short-form cut-downs for Instagram, LinkedIn, YouTube Shorts and TikTok
  • Proof series: exits, distributions paid, asset class vs S&P in down quarters
  • Founder and expert content on LinkedIn

Answer-engine and comparison content

When someone asks Google or ChatGPT how to invest in collectibles, Timeless is the answer.

  • Comparison pages: Timeless vs Splint, Konvi, Masterworks, ETFs, physical buying
  • Guides by asset class: watches, cars, art, wine, trading cards
  • German-first, structured for AI search and classic SEO

Phase 2 gives the bond its own audience. An ISIN can sit in a portfolio an adviser builds. That is the channel paid media never had. Collectible Invst is sold on portfolio fit and on the asset class, never on individual assets.

Adviser and distribution partner programme

  • Independent financial advisers, wealth managers and family offices in DACH
  • Broker and platform listings for the ISIN
  • Adviser kit, adviser events and webinars

Content

  • Collectibles as an uncorrelated allocation
  • Annual payouts as the portfolio mechanic
  • Clear risk language: illiquid, eight-year term, subordinated

Private Collector Club (optional)

  • Malte's idea: shared high-value deals for the top investors
  • Zero acquisition spend
  • Monetises the top 2,500 and creates retail deal flow

Phase 2 targets

20+ adviser and distribution partners onboarded. A majority of Collectible Invst subscriptions from investors new to Timeless. Average ticket at or above €5,000. Timeline: months 3 to 5. Costed in the Playbook.

One new EU market entered properly rather than three entered thinly, chosen on the evidence from Phases 1 and 2. Central and Southern Europe are the candidates. The UK and US stay out on regulatory and marketing grounds, and Asia and the UAE need individual regulatory assessments.
